**The gaps we are taking.** *One short paragraph naming the two or three openings the outline is built around. Be specific about why the gap exists. A vendor cannot recommend against its own product; a generalist site has no hands-on authority; a thin page has no depth. That reasoning is what makes the gap defensible rather than wishful.*

## The angle

*One paragraph stating the claim the piece makes that competitors do not. It has to be something a reasonable person could disagree with. "We will cover X thoroughly" is not an angle.*

## For comparison and "tested" articles only

*Add this block whenever the piece claims first-hand testing. Specify it at outline stage, because retrofitting a methodology after drafting does not work.*

**Targets:** [what gets tested, and why each one was chosen. Authorized and reproducible wherever possible.]

**Criteria:** [what is measured. Only list criteria the test actually exercises.]

**Exclusions:** [what is not measured, stated plainly. An untested dimension named up front is credibility; one discovered by a reviewer is a problem.]

**Runs:** [how many, and how variance is reported]

**Correctness:** [what counts as correct, incorrect, and unsupported. The third category matters: a tool that has no field for something has not got it wrong.]

**Disclosure:** [if the publisher is in the comparison, how fairness was maintained, in specifics rather than assertion]
